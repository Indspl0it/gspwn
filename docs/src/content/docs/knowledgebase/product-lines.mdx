NVIDIA sells the same architecture into three segments. Two independent axes
decide what a given part can do. The architecture generation sets the compute
feature set. The segment sets partitioning, reliability and support terms.

## Naming

Data centre parts are named by a letter and a number. The letter is the initial
of the architecture, which makes the generation readable from the part name.

| Letter | Architecture | Named after | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `K` | Kepler | Johannes Kepler | K80 |
| `M` | Maxwell | James Clerk Maxwell | M60 |
| `P` | Pascal | Blaise Pascal | P100, P4 |
| `V` | Volta | Alessandro Volta | V100 |
| `T` | Turing | Alan Turing | T4 |
| `A` | Ampere | André-Marie Ampère | A100, A10, A40 |
| `H` | Hopper | Grace Hopper | H100, H200 |
| `L` | Ada Lovelace | Ada Lovelace | L4, L40S |
| `B` | Blackwell | David Blackwell | B100, B200 |

Consumer parts use the `GeForce RTX` name with a generation-leading number:
the RTX 20 series is Turing, 30 is Ampere, 40 is Ada Lovelace, 50 is Blackwell.
Workstation parts were `Quadro` through Turing and are now `RTX A`, `RTX Ada`
or `RTX PRO`.

`Tesla` was the data centre brand name through Volta and is no longer used:
the A100 shipped as `NVIDIA A100`. `nvidia-smi` still reports older parts under
the old brand, as `Tesla T4`.

## Architecture generations

Compute capability is the identifier the toolchain uses for the generation. It
gates language and hardware features, so it appears in `nvcc` flags as
`-arch=sm_XX`.

| Generation | Compute capability | Examples | Notable additions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Volta | 7.0 | V100 | First tensor cores, independent thread scheduling |
| Turing | 7.5 | T4, RTX 20 series | Ray tracing cores, INT8 and INT4 tensor operations |
| Ampere | 8.0, 8.6 | A100 at 8.0; A10G, A40 and RTX 30 series at 8.6 | TF32, BF16, structured sparsity, asynchronous copy, MIG |
| Ada Lovelace | 8.9 | L4, L40S, RTX 40 series | FP8 tensor operations, shader execution reordering |
| Hopper | 9.0 | H100, H200 | Thread block clusters, the tensor memory accelerator, transformer engine |
| Blackwell | 10.x, 12.x | B200 at 10.x; RTX 50 series at 12.x | FP4 and FP6 tensor operations, second generation transformer engine |

Compiled device code carries the capability it was built for. Binary
compatibility holds within a major version at an equal or higher minor
version, so a cubin built for `sm_80` runs on 8.6 and 8.9 and fails on 7.5. A
binary that also embeds PTX can be compiled just in time for a capability that
did not exist when it was built.

## Memory and form factor

The form factor decides power, interconnect and cooling, and follows from the
segment more than from the generation.

| Form factor | Interconnect | Memory | Cooling | Typical parts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SXM module | NVLink to its neighbours, plus PCIe to the host | HBM | Passive, chassis-provided | A100, H100, B200 |
| PCIe card, data centre | PCIe, with an optional NVLink bridge | HBM or GDDR | Passive, chassis-provided | A100 PCIe, L40S, T4 |
| PCIe card, workstation | PCIe | GDDR with ECC on some parts | Active blower | RTX A6000, RTX PRO |
| PCIe card, consumer | PCIe | GDDR | Active, multi-fan | GeForce RTX |

HBM is stacked beside the die on the same package and carries far higher
bandwidth per watt than GDDR. Its power and thermal envelope puts the
highest-bandwidth parts on chassis-cooled modules and keeps them off
self-cooled cards.

## Instance families

Rented instances are advertised by family. The GPU each family carries is
fixed.

| Instance family | GPU | Generation | Form factor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `p3` | V100 | Volta | SXM |
| `g4dn` | T4 | Turing | PCIe |
| `g5` | A10G | Ampere | PCIe |
| `g6` | L4 | Ada Lovelace | PCIe |
| `p4d` | A100 | Ampere | SXM |
| `p5` | H100 | Hopper | SXM |

The `g` families are graphics and inference parts on PCIe. The `p` families are
training parts on SXM modules with NVLink between them.
